What Is Cabinet Drying?

Cabinet drying is a method of drying water trapped under and behind kitchen or bathroom cabinets by removing the toe-kick or drilling small hidden ports and pushing warm, dry air underneath, without removing cabinets or countertops. It works best on sudden clean-water leaks caught early.

Cabinet drying at a glance:

  • Best for: sink lines, RO filters, dishwashers, ice makers, disposals
  • Countertops: usually stay in place
  • Typical drying time: 3 to 5 days; 5 to 8 days if hardwood also needs mat drying
  • Biggest risk: particleboard swelling, which is permanent
  • Typical estimate: roughly $1,000 to $2,500 for the sink base and neighboring cabinets

Water Under Your Kitchen Cabinets? Do These 5 Things

  • Shut off the water at the source. Close the valves under the sink or behind the fridge. If they won't close, shut the main.
  • Empty the sink base. Take everything out and leave the doors open.
  • Unplug the disposal and dishwasher. Then switch off their breakers if water reached the outlets.
  • Don't pry off the toe-kick yourself. It can hide screws and shims that keep cabinets level.
  • Photograph the leak. Get the cracked hose, wet shelf and floor before anything is moved.

Most under-cabinet leaks come from a small set of fittings and appliances.

Why Water Hides Under Cabinets

Cabinets sit on a hollow base that traps water where fans can't reach it.

Cabinets Sit on a Hollow Frame

Base cabinets and islands rest on a hollow toe-kick frame. Water runs along grout lines and the slope of the slab right under the trim.

The Back Wall Soaks From the Bottom

Water wicks into the drywall behind the cabinets and into the bottom wood plate of the wall, where kitchen fans don't reach.

Particleboard Swells Fast

Many cabinet bottoms and sides are particleboard. Once it swells, it stays swollen, so speed matters.

The Island Gets Wet Too

Water crossing the kitchen floor often reaches the island's toe-kick, so it's checked and dried with the wall cabinets.

How Cabinets Are Dried Without Tearing Them Out

In-place cabinet drying follows five steps, used whenever moisture readings show it will work. Work follows recognized restoration practices (IICRC S500 guidance).

  • Map every cabinet run. Thermal cameras and probes check the sink base, neighboring cabinets, the dishwasher bay, the island and the wall behind them.
  • Open the toe-kick or drill hidden ports. The thin trim at the floor comes off, or small holes are drilled where they won't be seen.
  • Remove trapped water. Water pooled on the slab under the cabinets is extracted. See water extraction.
  • Push dry air underneath and behind. Tubes connected to a dehumidifier send warm, dry air under every cabinet box and up the back wall. See wall cavity drying.
  • Check the back wall daily. Insulated pin probes reach the drywall and wood plate behind the cabinets through the small ports, so dryness is confirmed without removing cabinets.

When Only the Sink Base Floor Is Ruined

If only the sink base floor panel sagged, just that panel can be cut out to clean and dry the slab and wall underneath, then replaced with a matching panel by a carpenter.

Cabinet Drying FAQs

Can cabinets really be dried without removing them or the granite?

Yes, on most sudden clean-water leaks from a sink line, RO filter, ice maker or dishwasher. The toe-kick comes off or small ports are drilled, pooled water is removed, and dry air is pushed under the cabinets and up the back wall.

The bottom of my sink cabinet is warped. Do all the cabinets have to go?

No. Usually just that bottom panel is cut out, the area underneath is cleaned and dried, and a matching panel is installed.

Is water under my island too?

Often, if water crossed the floor. The island sits on the same slab with the same hollow toe-kick, so it's checked and dried too.

How do you know the wall behind the cabinets is dry?

Insulated pin probes read moisture only at the tip, so they can measure the drywall and wood plate behind cabinets through small ports.

How long does cabinet drying take?

Most kitchen cabinet drying takes 3 to 5 days, or 5 to 8 days if hardwood also needs mat drying.

Can we use the kitchen while it dries?

Usually, yes. The equipment is compact and counters stay usable. The sink may be off for a day or two while it's repaired.

What are signs of water under kitchen cabinets?

Swollen toe-kicks, a musty smell from the sink base, soft or stained cabinet floors, warped flooring near cabinets, or peeling paint at the bottom of the wall. For urgent help, start with 24/7 emergency water damage cleanup.
