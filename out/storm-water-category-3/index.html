Storm Water in Your Plano Home: Why Outside Water Is Category 3 Black Water

It's just rainwater, right? That's what most Plano homeowners think when storm water slips under the back door or seeps through the brick. But the moment rain crosses your lawn, patio, mulch or street, it picks up bacteria, chemicals and dirt. Here's why that changes the cleanup, and how to do it safely.

Is Storm Water in My House Dangerous?

Yes. Under IICRC S500 guidance, water that enters a home from the ground outside is Category 3 (grossly contaminated), the same class as sewage, because it carries soil bacteria, animal waste, fertilizer, pesticides and street runoff. That means porous materials it touched usually must be removed, and surfaces must be cleaned and disinfected before drying.

Key points:

  • The myth: "It's clean rain. I'll vacuum it and run fans."
  • The reality: outside water is contaminated as soon as it crosses the ground.
  • Why fans are a bad idea first: blowing air over contaminated carpet and walls can spread bacteria and spores into the air.

Why Plano Homes Take On Storm Water

Plano homes take on storm water because of creeks running through the city, slabs that sit only inches above the yard, saturated clay soil and brick weep holes.

Creeks Run Through the City

Spring Creek, Rowlett Creek, White Rock Creek, Russell Creek and Prairie Creek cross Plano. In heavy storms, they and the street drains feeding them can back up into low streets, alley garages and backyards.

Slabs Sit Just Inches Above the Yard

Many Plano homes sit only a few inches above the lawn. Clogged gutters, a yard sloping toward the house, or an overwhelmed French drain let water pool against the wall until it rises over the slab edge.

Saturated Clay Can't Absorb More

After a few days of rain, Blackland Prairie clay is full and runoff heads toward the foundation.

Brick Weep Holes

Brick homes have small openings at the bottom row of brick to let moisture out. When water pools against the wall, it can flow in through those holes, over the slab edge and into the room.

Safe Cleanup Steps

Safe storm water cleanup follows five steps, in this order.

  • Kill power to wet rooms before stepping in.
  • Keep kids and pets out of the wet area.
  • Photograph the water line inside and outside for your claim.
  • Don't run fans until contaminated materials are removed and surfaces disinfected.
  • Get Category 3 cleanup: extraction, removal, disinfecting and drying. See flood cleanup.

After removal and disinfecting, dehumidifiers and air movers dry everything until studs read about 12 to 15%. See structural drying.

Will Insurance Cover Storm Water?

Rising surface water and creek flooding usually need a separate flood policy, while wind-driven rain through a storm-damaged roof or window is often covered by a homeowners policy. Photos showing exactly how water got in make a big difference. See the Texas water damage insurance guide.

Preventing the Next Storm Leak

  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear, and extend downspouts away from the house.
  • Make sure soil slopes away from the foundation.
  • Clean patio and yard drains before spring storm season.
  • Don't pile mulch against the brick or over weep holes.
  • Consider flood insurance if you're near a creek, even outside a mapped flood zone.

Storm Water FAQs

It was clean rain. Why treat it like sewage?

Once rain runs across lawn, patio, mulch or gutters, it picks up bacteria, animal waste and chemicals. That's why outside water is Category 3.

How did water get through my brick wall?

Through the weep holes at the bottom row of brick, when water pooled against the house.

Can I just pull the wet pad and run fans?

No. Wet insulation inside the wall and contaminated surfaces need removal and disinfecting first. See why fans won't dry walls.

My house isn't in a flood zone. Can it still flood?

Yes. Homes near creeks can get water from overflowing drains and sheet flow even outside mapped flood zones.

What is Category 3 water?

Category 3 is grossly contaminated water that can contain bacteria, viruses and chemicals, including sewage and outside flood water. It requires removal of porous materials it touched and disinfecting before drying.

How fast should I act?

Right away. Contamination and moisture get worse with time, and mold can start within a couple of days. See the mold clock guide.

Can I clean it myself?

Small amounts on tile can be cleaned carefully. Anything that soaked carpet, drywall or insulation should be handled with containment and protective gear. See sewage backup cleanup.
