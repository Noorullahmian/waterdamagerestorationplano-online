What Is Water Damage Restoration?

Water damage restoration in Plano, TX is the process of removing water from a home or business, measuring hidden moisture, and drying the structure back to normal moisture levels before mold or permanent damage sets in. It has three core stages: water extraction, structural drying with dehumidifiers and air movers, and daily moisture monitoring until materials read dry.

Water Emergency in Plano? Do These 5 Things in the Next 15 Minutes

  • Shut off the main water. In many Plano homes built between 1975 and 2005, the main valve is on the front garage wall or in a round box near the front flowerbed. If it won't turn, close the City of Plano meter valve at the curb box.
  • Cut power to wet rooms. Switch off breakers for any room where water reaches outlets, baseboards or ceiling lights. Never walk through standing water to reach the panel.
  • Isolate the water heater. If an attic or garage tank is leaking, turn the cold-water valve on top clockwise, then set the gas control to off or flip the 240V breaker.
  • Stay clear of a bulging ceiling. A water bubble in ceiling paint can let go without warning. Move people and furniture out, put a bucket under it and step back.
  • Record it before you move it. Take a 60-second video and wide photos of the water line, every wet room and the failed pipe, hose or tank. Your adjuster will ask for exactly this.

The Water You Can See Is Only Part of the Problem

Most water from a leak spreads into places you can't see, which is why mopping and box fans rarely finish the job.

  • Under the floor: Water slips past baseboards and gets trapped under LVP foam, hardwood adhesive or carpet pad. It can't evaporate through vinyl or finished wood, so it sits on the slab.
  • Up the walls: Drywall wicks water well above the visible line within hours. The bottom plate and wall insulation stay wet long after the paint feels dry.
  • Between floors: In two-story homes, an upstairs overflow drains into the cavity above the downstairs ceiling. Sealed in, humidity climbs and mold can start in 24 to 48 hours.
  • In the air: Once indoor humidity passes about 70%, dry doors, trim and cabinets start absorbing moisture. Doors stick and finishes crack where water never touched.

Why Box Fans and a Shop-Vac Aren't Enough

A shop-vac can't squeeze water out of dense carpet pad, and fans just move wet air around a closed house. Leave water behind and drying can stretch from a few days to well over a week. Our 5-Step Water Damage Restoration Process

Water damage restoration follows five steps: inspect and map moisture, extract standing water, classify the water, set up drying equipment, and monitor daily until materials read dry. Drying follows recognized restoration practices (IICRC S500 guidance), and every step is measured.

  • Inspect and map the moisture. Confirm the source is stopped, check for electrical hazards, then scan floors, walls and ceilings with thermal cameras and moisture meters. Readings are recorded before anything is moved.
  • Extract the standing water. Truck-mounted vacuums, submersible pumps for deep water and weighted extractors that press water out of carpet and pad.
  • Classify the water. Category 1 (clean supply line), Category 2 (washer, dishwasher, water heater) or Category 3 (sewage, creek or storm runoff). The category decides what can dry in place and what must come out.
  • Set up the drying. Dehumidifiers and air movers are sized to the space and how wet it is. Walls, cabinets and hardwood get targeted cavity drying or drying mats.
  • Monitor daily until dry. Temperature, humidity, grains per pound and wood moisture are logged every day. Equipment stays until framing reads in the dry range (about 12 to 15% for wood in North Texas).

Typical drying time: 3 to 5 days for a clean-water leak caught early; 5 to 8 days when water reached hardwood, cabinets or the space between floors.

Water is coming up through my floor. Who do I call?

That's often a slab leak: a pinhole in a copper line under the concrete, frequently linked to shifting clay soil. Shut off the main water and call us. We dry what's under the floor and work alongside your plumber, who repairs the pipe.

My attic water heater leaked through the ceiling. Can the ceiling be saved?

Often, yes, if the soaked insulation is pulled off the drywall on day one. That weight is what makes ceilings sag and fall. Removed quickly, many ceilings dry in place in 2 to 3 days. Until then, follow these safety steps for a sagging wet ceiling.

How long does it take to dry a house after a leak?

A clean-water leak caught early usually reaches dry in 3 to 5 days. Water under hardwood, cabinets or between floors can take 5 to 8 days.

How soon can mold grow after water damage?

Mold can start growing on wet drywall, wood and carpet pad within 24 to 48 hours, especially in Plano's warm months. Fast extraction and drying is the best prevention.

Can I turn the fans and dehumidifiers off at night?

Please don't. Turning them off lets humidity climb back up, drying stalls, and mold gets the damp window it needs.

Can my family stay home during drying?

For most clean-water losses, yes; the drying area is sectioned off. For sewage backups, or older homes that may need asbestos or lead precautions, staying out of the work area is safer.
