What Is Wall Cavity Drying?

Wall cavity drying is a method of drying water trapped inside a wall by removing the baseboard, drilling small holes that the baseboard will hide, and blowing warm, dry air into each stud bay. A flood cut, which removes the bottom 12 or 24 inches of drywall, is used only when water was dirty, insulation is soaked, or drywall is damaged.

Wall drying at a glance:

  • Works for: clean-water leaks in uninsulated interior walls with firm drywall
  • Flood cut needed for: sewage or outside water, soaked insulation, softened or moldy drywall
  • Last part to dry: the bottom wood plate on the slab
  • Dry target: about 12 to 15% for wood framing
  • Typical drying time: 2 to 4 days for interior walls
  • Typical estimate: roughly $500 to $1,500 for one room of interior walls with hidden drying

Think Your Walls Got Wet? Check These 5 Things

  • Press the wall a couple of inches above the baseboard. Soft, cool or damp drywall, yellowing caulk or swollen trim joints mean water got in.
  • Don't caulk or paint over swollen baseboards. Sealing the top locks moisture inside and invites mold.
  • Don't rely on box fans. Paint slows evaporation, and air in the room can't dry water sitting on the wood plate inside the wall.
  • Keep outlets off on wet walls. Water can wick up to outlet height.
  • Get walls checked within 24 to 48 hours. When water touches a baseboard, it usually reaches the wood plate under the drywall.

How Water Gets Inside Plano Walls

Water gets into walls through the gap under the drywall, then wicks upward into drywall and insulation.

The Hidden Gap Under Your Drywall

In slab homes, drywall is hung with a small gap at the bottom, covered by the baseboard. Even shallow water on the floor runs under the baseboard and collects along the bottom wood plate.

Drywall Wicks Water Up

From that plate, paper-faced drywall wicks water upward over hours, often well above the visible water line.

Insulation Soaks It Up

Exterior brick walls, and many bathroom and bedroom walls insulated for sound, are filled with insulation that holds water behind the paint.

MDF Baseboards Swell

Many painted baseboards are MDF with bare bottoms and backs, so they soak up water and swell at the joints.

Dry It or Cut It? How the Decision Is Made

Three questions decide whether a wall can be dried through hidden holes or needs a flood cut: was the water clean, is there wet insulation, and is the drywall still sound? This follows recognized restoration practices (IICRC S500 guidance).

When Hidden Wall Drying Works

  • The water was clean, such as a supply line or water heater.
  • It's an uninsulated interior wall, or only the very bottom of an exterior wall got damp.
  • The drywall is still firm, with no swelling, blistering or mold.

The baseboard is removed carefully, small holes are drilled below the baseboard line, and warm, dry air is pushed into every stud bay. When the baseboard goes back on, it covers the holes.

When a Flood Cut Is Required

  • Dirty water: sewage, a toilet overflow with waste, or outdoor flood water touched the drywall. See sewage backup cleanup and flood cleanup.
  • Soaked insulation: water climbed into insulation inside an exterior or sound wall. Air can't push through a wet batt, so the bottom of the wall has to open.
  • Damaged drywall: water sat 48+ hours and softened the core, or mold has started on the back.

A flood cut removes a straight strip, usually 12 or 24 inches off the floor, set above the highest wet reading.

The Wall Drying Process

Wall drying follows five steps, and a wet wall is never closed up.

  • Measure how high the water climbed. Thermal cameras and meters show how far it wicked. See moisture mapping.
  • Remove and number the baseboards. Caulk is scored first so paint doesn't tear. Solid wood trim is saved for reinstalling.
  • Look inside. A small hole behind the baseboard line shows whether there's insulation, what kind of water it was, and how wet the bottom plate is.
  • Dry it or cut it. Clean, uninsulated walls get hidden cavity drying. Dirty or insulation-filled walls get a level flood cut, wet insulation removed and studs cleaned.
  • Check the bottom plate daily. Drying ends only when it reads about 12 to 15%. See structural drying.

Wall Drying FAQs

How can walls be dried without cutting big holes?

On clean-water leaks with no wet insulation, the baseboard comes off, small holes go in where the baseboard covers them, and dry air is pushed into the wall. Then the baseboard goes back on.

Why cut at 12 or 24 inches?

The cut goes above the highest wet reading so all wet material comes out. Those heights also let standard 48-inch drywall sheets be cut cleanly in halves or quarters for the rebuild.

Why did my baseboards swell after a small spill?

They're likely MDF. The bare bottom and back absorb water and swell quickly.

Why is the bottom board of the wall always last to dry?

It sits flat on the cool slab, is often denser treated lumber, and collects water running down the wall.

Only a little water touched my exterior wall. Why is the insulation wet higher up?

Drywall and paper-faced insulation wick water upward, so a small amount on the floor can climb several inches inside the wall within hours.

How do I know if water got inside my wall?

Look for swollen or separating baseboards, bubbling paint, soft drywall near the floor, a musty smell, or cool spots on a thermal scan.

How long does wall drying take?

Usually 2 to 4 days for interior walls, longer if insulation had to come out. For active leaks, start with 24/7 emergency water damage cleanup.
