Why Is My Floor Warm in One Spot?

A warm spot on a first-floor slab usually means a pinhole leak in a hot-water copper line buried under the concrete, with hot water pooling beneath the floor. It's a common sign of a slab leak in Plano homes built on expansive clay.

The 2-minute test:

  • Turn off every faucet and water-using appliance.
  • Go to the water heater. Listen for a faint hiss or feel whether the outlet pipe stays hot.
  • Close the cold-water valve on top of the tank.
  • If the hiss stops, you've likely isolated a hot-water slab leak.

Why it's urgent: even after the plumber fixes the pipe, water already trapped under the flooring can buckle wood and start mold within about 48 hours.

Why Warm Spots Happen So Often in Plano

Warm floor spots are common in Plano because shifting clay, hot-line wear and aging copper combine under many homes.

Expansive Clay Moves the Slab

Plano sits on Blackland Prairie clay that swells in wet seasons and shrinks in dry ones. The slab flexes with it, and buried copper lines can rub against concrete and gravel until they wear through. See copper pinhole leaks.

Hot Lines Often Fail First

Hot-water lines expand and contract every time hot water runs, and heat speeds up wear from water chemistry. That's why many Plano slab leaks are on the hot side.

The Age of Your House

Homes built from the 1970s through the late 1990s in areas like Hunters Glen, Park Forest, Whiffletree, Russell Creek and Willow Bend often have copper lines under the slab that are now decades old.

Why the Plumber Only Fixes Half the Problem

Your plumber stops new water with a reroute, bypass or tunnel repair, but the water that already leaked stays under your floors, inside walls and under cabinets.

  • Under hardwood and LVP: water spreads across the slab under the vapor barrier or foam, where it can't evaporate.
  • Inside walls: it soaks the bottom wood plate behind kitchen and bathroom cabinets.
  • Under cabinets: it pools in the hollow toe-kick spaces.

That trapped water is warm, dark and wet, ideal for mold. It's why floors can keep cupping after the repair. See the mold clock guide.

How Under-Floor Drying Works

Under-floor drying has four parts: mapping, floor drying, wall and cabinet drying, and slab drying.

  • Thermal mapping: a thermal camera shows the wet footprint, which is often larger than the warm spot you felt. See moisture mapping.
  • Floor-by-floor method: hardwood gets vacuum drying mats; LVP is lifted so water under the foam can be removed. See hardwood floor drying.
  • Walls and cabinets: small hidden ports let dry air reach the bottom of walls and under cabinets. See cabinet drying.
  • Slab drying: dehumidifiers run until slab readings return to normal, so new flooring adhesive won't fail later.

What Not to Do

  • Don't pry up warm, cupping boards. They're under pressure and can split the whole run.
  • Don't cover the spot with a rug. It traps rising moisture and speeds up mold.
  • Don't install new flooring until the slab is tested dry.

Warm Spot on Floor FAQs

Why is my floor warm in one spot?

In a slab home, a warm spot usually means hot water is leaking from a buried pipe under that area.

Can a warm spot be something other than a leak?

Sometimes a hot-water line running close under the slab warms the floor slightly without leaking. A leak detection test and moisture scan confirm which it is.

Should I keep using hot water?

No. Close the cold-water valve on top of the water heater until a plumber checks it. Cold water can usually stay on.

How do I check my water meter for a leak?

Turn off all water inside and out, then look at the curb meter's leak indicator (a small triangle or dial). If it moves, water is escaping somewhere. See the shut-off valve guide.

My plumber rerouted the line. Why are my floors still cupping?

The water that leaked before the repair is still under the floor and needs mats and dehumidifiers to come out. See slab leak water damage.

Does insurance cover slab leaks?

Resulting water damage is often covered when the leak was hidden and sudden, while the pipe repair and slab access may be handled differently. See the Texas water damage insurance guide.

How long does under-floor drying take?

Usually 4 to 7 days, longer with hardwood.
