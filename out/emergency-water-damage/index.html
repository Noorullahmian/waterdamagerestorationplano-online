What Is Emergency Water Damage Cleanup?

Emergency water damage cleanup is the immediate response to an active leak or flood: stopping the water, making the home safe, extracting standing water and starting structural drying within hours, before water spreads into walls, subfloors and ceilings. In Plano, TX, it covers burst pipes, attic water heater failures, AC overflows, appliance leaks, sewage backups and storm water, 24 hours a day.

Before Help Arrives: Your 15-Minute Emergency Checklist

  • Close the main water valve. Check the front wall inside the garage, a utility closet, or the round box near the front flowerbed. If the house valve won't fully close, shut the City of Plano meter at the curb with a curb key.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms. Flip breakers for any room with wet ceilings, dripping light fixtures or water near baseboard outlets.
  • Lift furniture off wet carpet. Slide foil, plastic lids or wood blocks under sofa and table legs so rust and wood stain don't bleed into the carpet.
  • Block the spread. Roll towels along dry hallway thresholds and doorways to slow water moving into more rooms.
  • Skip the household vacuum. Upright vacuums and ungrounded shop-vacs are a shock risk on wet floors and can't reach water under the carpet pad.

Why the First Few Hours Decide Your Repair Bill

The first few hours decide whether a water leak becomes a 3-day dry-out or a multi-week tear-out, because water keeps moving into drywall, insulation and subfloor the longer it sits. Most Plano emergencies don't happen at 10 AM on a Tuesday: attic water heater relief valves fail overnight, pipes split the morning after a hard freeze, and storms roll through while families are at work.

What Happens on the First Emergency Visit

A proper first emergency visit covers five things: safety, moisture mapping, water extraction, opening trapped moisture, and starting drying. Work follows recognized water damage restoration practices (IICRC S500 guidance).

  • Safety first. Confirm the water source is isolated, check wet walls and standing water for electrical hazards, and safely brace or bring down ceilings bulging with water.
  • Map the damage before touching it. Thermal cameras and moisture meters trace how far water traveled across floors, into walls and between floors. Readings are photographed for your claim.
  • Pull out the water. Truck-mounted vacuums and weighted extractors remove standing water and press free water out of carpet and pad. More on standing water extraction in Plano.
  • Open up trapped moisture. Baseboards are carefully removed (caulk scored first so paint doesn't tear), small vent holes go behind the trim line, and plastic containment seals off wet rooms.
  • Start drying right away. Dehumidifiers and air movers start on the first visit, with antimicrobial applied to exposed framing where needed. Daily checks follow until the home reads dry. See structural drying in Plano.

Do I Need to Wait for My Insurance Adjuster?

No. Texas homeowners policies generally expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage right away, often called the duty to mitigate. The untouched scene is photographed and thermal images and moisture readings are recorded before work starts, so your adjuster still gets clear evidence of the sudden water event. Coverage always depends on your own policy, so confirm with your insurance company.

Emergency Water Damage Cleanup FAQs

Can I really call at 2 AM or on a Sunday?

Yes. (972) 472-7917 takes calls 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including holidays. Arrival time depends on your location and call volume; you'll get an estimate when you call.

What does the first emergency visit include?

Water source and electrical safety checks, thermal and moisture mapping, standing water extraction, removal of soaked pad or collapsing ceiling drywall, antimicrobial treatment where needed, and setup of dehumidifiers and air movers.

What should I do first when a pipe bursts or a water heater leaks?

Shut off the main water valve, cut power to wet rooms, move valuables, and photograph everything before moving it. Then call (972) 472-7917. If you can't find your valve, use the Plano main water shut-off guide.

Can my family stay in the house tonight?

For most clean-water leaks, yes. Wet rooms are sealed off and the rest of the house stays livable. For sewage backups, or pre-1978 homes needing asbestos or lead precautions, it's safer to stay out of the work area.

Why was the bottom of my drywall cut?

When water wicks high into a wall or is contaminated, removing the bottom 12 to 24 inches (a flood cut) exposes wet studs and insulation so they can dry quickly and safely.

Will my baseboards and paint be ruined?

Usually not. The caulk line is scored before trim comes off, so solid wood baseboards can be numbered, dried and reinstalled without tearing wall paint.

Do you fix the leaking pipe too?

The water side is handled here: stopping the spread, extraction and drying. Your plumber repairs the pipe, and both can happen at the same time so nothing waits.

How soon does mold start after a leak?

Mold can begin growing on wet drywall, wood and carpet pad within 24 to 48 hours, which is why emergency extraction and drying matter so much.
