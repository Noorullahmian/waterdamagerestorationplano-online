What Is Commercial Water Damage Restoration?

Commercial water damage restoration is the extraction, drying and documentation of water damage in offices, medical suites, retail stores, restaurants, warehouses and multi-unit buildings, scheduled to limit business downtime. It differs from residential work because of steel stud walls, glue-down flooring, multi-story water travel, multiple tenants and multiple insurance policies.

Commercial water damage at a glance:

  • Property types: offices, medical and dental suites, retail, restaurants, warehouses, apartments
  • Plano business areas: Legacy West, Granite Park, Headquarters Drive, Parker Road medical corridor, Jupiter Road, US-75
  • Scheduling: after-hours and weekend work to keep operations running
  • Common sources: breakroom lines, restroom valves, HVAC units, sprinkler heads, roof leaks
  • Documentation: photos, thermal images, moisture maps, daily logs, itemized Xactimate-format scope
  • Typical estimate: roughly $3,000 to $8,000 for a single small suite with clean water

Water in Your Office, Store or Clinic? Do These 5 Things

  • Shut the suite or riser valve. In office towers and retail centers, the suite valve is often above the ceiling grid. Building engineering can close the floor riser.
  • Don't touch the fire sprinkler riser yourself. If a sprinkler head is discharging, call 911 or your fire monitoring company so the system is shut down correctly.
  • Protect equipment. Power down servers, POS terminals and medical devices. Lift UPS batteries off the floor and cover racks with plastic.
  • Pull wet ceiling tiles over walkways. Soaked acoustic tiles sag and can fall onto desks and aisles without warning.
  • Block off slip zones. Put cones at suite entries and elevator lobbies to protect staff, patients and customers.

Commercial buildings use different materials, spread water across multiple floors and tenants, and can't afford long closures, so they need a different drying plan than homes. Steel Studs and Glue-Down Flooring

Offices and retail suites use steel studs, fire-rated drywall, rubber cove base and glue-down carpet or vinyl tile over concrete. Water behaves differently in these materials than in a wood-framed house.

Multi-Story Water Travels Down

When a breakroom ice maker, restroom flush valve or HVAC unit fails upstairs, water spreads across the floor deck and runs down chases, shafts and window mullions into suites below.

Downtime Is the Real Cost

Medical and dental offices, restaurants and corporate offices can't close for a week with loud equipment in the halls. Commercial jobs need phased after-hours work, contained drying and work zones that respect patient privacy and safety.

Where Water Hides in Commercial Buildings

In commercial spaces, water hides in steel stud tracks, under glue-down carpet, above ceiling grids and in concrete floor decks.

  • Inside steel stud tracks: the bottom track acts like a gutter, holding water against drywall and insulation until the wall is vented.
  • Under glue-down carpet: water spreads under the backing and can release the adhesive if it sits.
  • Above the ceiling grid: insulation, ductwork and cabling hold water after the tiles are pulled.
  • In the floor deck: concrete holds moisture that can ruin new flooring if it isn't dried.

The Commercial Water Damage Process

Commercial jobs are planned around your business in five steps. Work follows recognized restoration practices (IICRC S500 guidance).

  • Walkthrough with your facility team. Wet areas across every affected suite and floor are mapped with thermal cameras and meters, and a schedule is agreed to keep you open where possible.
  • Fast extraction, after hours when needed. Truck-mounted units and extractors clear standing water from carpet, tile and concrete. See water extraction.
  • Contain and protect. Plastic containment and HEPA air scrubbers seal work zones so the rest of the building keeps running.
  • Open walls only where needed. Cove base comes off, steel stud walls are vented or cut low, and wet ceiling tiles and insulation are removed.
  • Dry and log daily. Commercial dehumidifiers and air movers run until floors, walls and decks reach dry standard, with daily logs for your property manager and insurer. See structural drying.

Commercial Water Damage FAQs

Can the work happen after hours so we stay open?

Yes. Extraction and noisy setup can happen overnight or on weekends, with quieter equipment and containment during business hours.

A sprinkler head went off in our suite. What now?

Call 911 or your fire monitoring company to shut the system down safely, then call (972) 472-7917. Sprinkler water often comes from stagnant pipes, so carpet and ceiling materials need careful handling.

Water came down from the floor above. Who handles that?

Multiple suites and floors can be dried in one coordinated job, with separate documentation for each owner, tenant and insurer.

Can glue-down office carpet be dried in place?

Often, on clean water caught quickly. Weighted extraction and drying can save it before the adhesive lets go.

Do you handle medical and dental offices?

Yes. Work zones are contained, equipment is placed to protect patient areas, and scheduling is coordinated with your office manager around privacy and appointments.

How long does commercial drying take?

Most clean-water commercial losses dry in about 3 to 5 days. Large or multi-floor losses take longer and are scheduled in phases.

Who should a business call first after a commercial water leak?

Building engineering or the property manager to stop the source, then a water damage restoration team to start extraction. For urgent help, see 24/7 emergency water damage cleanup.
