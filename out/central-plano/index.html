Water Emergency in Your Central Plano Home? Do These 5 Things

  • Clean supply water or a drain backup? If dirty water is rising from a shower drain or toilet, stop all water use in the house right away.
  • Shut the main valve. In many ranch homes, look for a brass valve on the garage's front wall or use a key at the curb box.
  • Go easy on old valves. Original wheel-style gate valves can snap if forced. If it resists, close the city meter instead.
  • Turn off hallway circuits. Central hallways often group the hall bath, utility closet and AC return together.
  • Lift furniture off wet carpet. Foil squares under wood furniture legs prevent stain from bleeding into carpet.

Why Central Plano Homes Get Water Damage

Central Plano homes get water damage mainly from aging copper and cast-iron under the slab, indoor atrium drains that clog in storms, and creek and street runoff.

Copper Supply and Cast-Iron Drains

Homes from the 1970s and early 80s, especially in 75075, may still have cast-iron sewer lines under the slab. As clay shifts, cast iron can sag, rust and back up into baths, while corroded copper hot-water lines can leak under kitchen and hallway floors. See slab leak water damage and sewage backup cleanup.

The Atrium Flood

Many 1970s and 80s ranch homes have an open-air atrium or courtyard in the middle of the house, drained by a single pipe. When it clogs in a storm, the atrium fills and water pours through sliding doors into the house. That water is Category 3. See flood cleanup.

Creek and Street Runoff

Homes near Spring Creek, Pitman Creek and Prairie Creek can see street gutters and alley garages overflow in heavy rain.

Central Plano Water Damage FAQs

Our indoor atrium flooded into the living room. How is that cleaned up?

The atrium is pumped out, the rooms are extracted, contaminated pad and baseboards come out, wall bottoms are cleaned, and the slab is disinfected and dried.

Why do 1970s and 80s homes here get both slab leaks and sewer backups?

They were often built with soft copper supply lines and cast-iron drains under the slab. Either one can fail as clay moves and pipes age.

Can bathroom tile be saved after a cast-iron backup?

Usually, if it's well bonded to the slab. It's deep-cleaned and disinfected, but baseboards and the bottom of the drywall still come out because sewage wicks under them.

Is the slab tested before new flooring goes in?

Yes. Daily slab and framing readings and a final dry reading help protect your new floor and its warranty.

Should we replace our cast-iron drains after a backup?

That's a plumber's call after a camera inspection. Many homeowners line or replace old cast iron once it starts backing up.

Our home has popcorn ceilings. Is that a problem?

Older popcorn ceilings may need asbestos testing before removal, and proper testing steps are followed before disturbing them.
