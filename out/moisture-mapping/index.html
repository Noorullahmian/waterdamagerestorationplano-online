What Is Moisture Mapping?

Moisture mapping is a non-invasive inspection that uses a thermal camera to find cooler, possibly wet areas behind walls, ceilings and floors, then confirms them with moisture meters and records the results on a floor plan. It shows exactly what's wet, how far water spread, and whether a home is truly dry.

Moisture mapping at a glance:

  • Finds: hidden leaks, wet drywall, wet insulation, water under floors
  • Tools: thermal camera, pinless meter, insulated pin probe, hygrometer
  • Damage to your home: none from scanning; pin probes leave tiny holes usually hidden at the baseboard
  • Result: a report with thermal images, photos, readings and a recommendation
  • Typical estimate: roughly $150 to $400 for one room or one suspected leak area

Before Your Moisture Scan: 5 Things That Help

  • Leave the thermostat steady for 2 hours before. Thermal cameras read small temperature differences, so steady air gives a clearer picture.
  • Don't cut holes to look. A scan covers large areas quickly without damage.
  • Skip bleach and air freshener on walls. Spraying a wall changes its temperature and can hide what the camera needs to see.
  • Pull furniture about 18 inches off suspect walls. The camera needs a clear view from floor to ceiling.
  • Have your water bill or plumber's report ready. Knowing where a leak was fixed helps map how far water traveled.

When Plano Homeowners Need a Moisture Scan

A moisture scan is useful any time you suspect hidden water or need proof a home is dry.

  • After a plumber or roofer leaves. The source is fixed, but walls, ceilings and floors may still be wet.
  • A musty smell with no visible stain. Especially after heavy rain, or near a bathroom or laundry.
  • A sudden jump in your water bill. Often a hidden slab or wall leak. See slab leak water damage.
  • Swollen baseboards, bubbling paint or cupping floors. Signs of water inside walls or under floors.
  • Checking a previous cleanup. Proof the home is dry before closing walls or laying new flooring.
  • Supporting an insurance claim. A clear moisture map shows your adjuster what got wet.

How Thermal Imaging Finds Hidden Water

As water evaporates, it cools the surface around it, so wet drywall, ceilings and floors show up as cooler patches on a thermal camera. That outline shows where water may be hiding behind paint, tile and wood.

A cool spot isn't always water, though. A cold air duct or a poorly insulated wall can look similar. That's why every thermal reading is checked with a moisture meter before calling it wet.

The Moisture Mapping Process

Moisture mapping follows five steps and ends with a written report. It follows recognized restoration inspection practices (IICRC S500 guidance).

  • Walk the house and set a baseline. Readings in an unaffected room show what "dry" means for your materials.
  • Scan every wall, ceiling and floor near the problem. The thermal camera finds cool, possibly wet zones.
  • Confirm with meters. Every suspect area is checked with pinless and pin meters.
  • Map it. Wet areas are marked on a floor plan with photos and readings.
  • Hand you the report. Thermal images, photos, readings and a clear recommendation: dry it in place, open it up, or no action needed.

Moisture Mapping FAQs

Can a thermal camera see through walls?

No. It reads surface temperature. Wet materials behind a wall cool the surface, and the camera picks that up. Meters then confirm it.

There's a musty smell but no stain. Can it be found?

Often, yes. Scanning rooms near bathrooms, laundry and exterior walls usually finds the cool, wet area causing the smell.

Do holes need to be cut?

No. Thermal scanning and pinless meters don't damage anything. Pin probes leave tiny holes that are usually hidden at the baseboard.

My plumber fixed the leak. How do I know the house is dry?

A scan shows whether walls, floors and ceilings still hold water. If they do, drying can start before mold does. See structural drying.

Will the report help my insurance claim?

Yes. Thermal images, photos and readings give your adjuster a clear record of what got wet and how far it spread.

Can the slab be checked before new flooring goes in?

Yes. Slab and subfloor readings before installation help protect your new flooring and its warranty.

What are signs of a hidden water leak in my home?

A musty smell, a rising water bill, warm or cool spots on the floor, swollen baseboards, bubbling paint, or a meter that moves when all water is off. For active leaks, start with 24/7 emergency water damage cleanup.
