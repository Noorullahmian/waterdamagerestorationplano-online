Water Emergency in Your McKinney Home? Do These 5 Things

  • Know your foundation. Near Historic Downtown (75069), your home may sit on piers with a crawl space, so cut the main breaker before looking under it. In Stonebridge Ranch, Craig Ranch or Trinity Falls, it's likely a slab.
  • Isolate a hot-water slab leak at the water heater. Close the cold-water valve on top of the tank.
  • Shut the curb meter or garage valve for a burst pipe.
  • Treat creek runoff as contaminated. Keep kids and pets out of outside water that came into a lower floor or garage.
  • Blot hardwood and lift furniture legs. Foil squares under legs on wet carpet prevent stains.

McKinney Water Damage FAQs

Why are slab leaks common in Stonebridge Ranch and Eldorado?

Like Plano, McKinney sits on expansive clay. Seasonal slab movement can wear buried copper hot lines until pinholes form.

Can a historic pier-and-beam home near the Square be dried?

Yes. The crawl space is pumped, fallen wet insulation is removed, wood is treated if a drain leaked, and the space is dried with dehumidifiers and protected with a vapor barrier.

How is the space between floors dried in Craig Ranch or Trinity Falls?

Thermal cameras trace the water, light trims come off to vent the cavity, and dry air runs across the joists until they read about 12 to 15%.

Does insurance cover a burst pipe or water heater in McKinney?

Sudden pipe and water heater failures are usually covered under standard Texas homeowners policies. Photos, drying logs and an itemized scope support the claim. Check your policy.

Are historic buildings around the McKinney Square handled carefully?

Yes. Brick, plaster and original wood are dried slowly, with openings only where truly needed.

Our Trinity Falls home is new. Can it still leak?

Yes. New slabs on expansive clay settle in the first few years, which can stress plumbing connections and shower pans.
