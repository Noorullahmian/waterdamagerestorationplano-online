How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Plano? (Quick Answer)

In Plano, small clean-water leaks caught fast often cost roughly $1,000 to $4,000 to dry out; multi-room losses with ceilings, insulation or cabinets often run roughly $3,000 to $9,000 for mitigation; and large, two-story or sewage losses can reach $8,000 to $20,000 or more, plus any rebuild. Speed is the biggest factor you control: water left 48+ hours often turns drying into tear-out.

All figures on this page are rough planning estimates, not quotes.

The 4 Things That Set the Price

Water damage pricing comes from four measurable factors: wet area, water category, how hard materials are to dry, and the number of drying days. Many insurance-backed jobs in Texas are priced in Xactimate, the estimating software widely used by insurers.

1. Square Footage and Room Size

More wet area means more extraction, more equipment and more monitoring.

2. Water Category

  • Category 1 (clean): supply lines, water heater supply side. Lowest cost.
  • Category 2 (gray): washers, dishwashers, AC overflow. Pad removal and antimicrobial added.
  • Category 3 (black): sewage and outside flood water. Containment and removal of porous materials add cost. See sewage backup cleanup.

3. How Hard It Is to Dry

Carpet and drywall dry fairly quickly. Hardwood, cabinets, plaster and slabs hold water deep inside and need specialty drying. See structural drying.

4. How Many Days

Equipment is charged per day, so the faster a home reaches dry standard, the lower the drying bill.

Why Calling on Day One Can Cut the Cost

Calling on day one keeps most materials dryable in place; waiting past 48 hours usually means more removal and higher rebuild costs.

  • Day one: most clean-water materials can be dried in place. Baseboards go back on, carpet is often saved.
  • Day two or three: pad, swollen trim and soaked insulation start needing replacement. Hardwood may cup.
  • After 48 hours: clean water can degrade to Category 2, mold can start, and more drywall, flooring and cabinets may need removal.

File a Claim or Pay Yourself?

Whether to file depends mainly on how the loss compares to your deductible. Many Texas policies use a percentage deductible, such as 1% of the home's insured value, or a fixed deductible like $1,000 to $2,500. On a $400,000 home with a 1% deductible, you'd pay the first $4,000 yourself.

  • Small, quick, clean leaks under your deductible are often paid directly.
  • Larger losses with ceilings, floors, cabinets or multiple rooms usually make more sense to file, especially when rebuild is needed.

This is general information, not financial or legal advice. Check your own policy and talk to your agent. See the Texas water damage insurance guide.

Hidden Costs to Watch For

  • Rebuild after drying: new drywall, paint, flooring and trim are separate from drying.
  • The plumbing or roof repair: handled by your plumber or roofer.
  • Contents: moving, cleaning or storing furniture can add cost on large losses.
  • After-hours response: nights and weekends may carry an emergency rate.
  • Testing: older homes may need asbestos testing before certain materials are removed.

Water Damage Cost FAQs

How much does water damage restoration cost in Plano?

Many clean-water jobs fall roughly between $1,000 and $9,000 for drying, depending on size. Sewage and large two-story losses run higher. An on-site inspection gives the real number.

Why can't I get an exact price on the phone?

Hidden moisture in walls, floors and ceilings decides the scope, and it can only be measured on site.

Does insurance pay for water damage restoration?

Sudden and accidental water damage is usually covered under standard Texas homeowners policies, minus your deductible. Check your own policy.

Is the plumber's bill part of the restoration cost?

No. The pipe or tank repair is separate from extraction and drying.

How much does it cost per day to run drying equipment?

Equipment is billed per day, and electricity for a typical home setup is usually a modest daily cost.

Will saving my hardwood really cost less?

Usually, yes. Mat drying typically costs a fraction of tearing out and replacing a connected wood floor. See hardwood floor drying.

What is the cheapest way to handle water damage?

Act within the first hours: shut off the water, extract it the same day, and dry materials in place before they need replacing.
