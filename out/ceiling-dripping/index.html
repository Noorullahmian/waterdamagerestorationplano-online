Why Is Water Dripping From My Ceiling?

In a Plano home, water dripping from the ceiling usually comes from one of four sources: an overflowing attic AC drain pan, a failed attic water heater, a roof leak, or a leaking upstairs bathroom or laundry. Weather, drip speed and which floor it's on tell you which one.

The 3 clues:

  • Is it raining? No rain and a hot afternoon usually means the attic AC or water heater, not the roof.
  • Fast and warm, or slow and cool? Fast, warm or rusty water: shut the cold-water valve on the attic water heater. Slow, cool drip near a hallway or vent: switch the thermostat from COOL to OFF.
  • Which floor? A first-floor ceiling in a two-story home has no attic above it, so the drip is coming from an upstairs toilet, shower, sink or washer.

Why Fixing the Source Doesn't Fix the Ceiling

When an attic AC or water heater leaks, the water soaks the thick blown-in insulation sitting on your ceiling drywall, and that wet insulation keeps damaging the ceiling after the source is fixed.

  • Wet insulation is heavy. It adds a lot of weight to the drywall.
  • The ceiling softens. Wet drywall loses strength and can pull off its screws.
  • Attic heat doesn't dry it. The top may dry, but the bottom stays wet against the cool ceiling.
  • Mold can start in about 48 hours, especially in wet cellulose.

So after the AC tech clears the drain or the plumber replaces the tank, the wet insulation still has to come out. See wet attic insulation removal.

What Happens When Help Arrives

Ceiling drying follows four steps.

  • Thermal scan from below shows the full wet area above the ceiling.
  • Wet insulation is vacuumed out of only the wet zone.
  • The top of the ceiling and joists are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Caught early, many ceilings dry in 2 to 3 days and need only paint touch-up.
  • Walls below are checked, because water often runs along the ceiling and drops inside a wall. See wall cavity drying.

Ceiling Dripping FAQs

Water is dripping from my ceiling but it's not raining. Why?

It's most likely the attic AC drain pan or the attic water heater. Check the thermostat and the water heater first.

Why is water coming out of my AC vent?

Either the attic pan overflowed and water ran along the duct to the vent, or hot attic air is condensing on a poorly insulated vent box.

Should I poke a hole in the ceiling bubble?

If it's a soft paint bubble over a dry floor, you can drain it into a bucket with a small poke. If the drywall itself is sagging, stay out from under it. See the sagging ceiling guide.

Is it safe to leave the lights on?

No. Turn off the switch and the breaker for any ceiling light near the drip.

My AC tech fixed the drain. Am I done?

Not if the insulation and ceiling are wet. They need drying or the ceiling can sag and grow mold.

Does insurance cover a dripping ceiling?

Sudden AC, water heater or storm-related leaks are often covered; slow, long-term leaks may not be. See the Texas water damage insurance guide.

What should I do first when water drips from the ceiling?

Turn off nearby lights at the breaker, put a bucket under the drip, shut off the likely source, and move belongings. For urgent help, see 24/7 emergency water damage cleanup.
