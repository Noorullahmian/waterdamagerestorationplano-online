What Is Structural Drying?

Structural drying is the process of removing absorbed moisture from a building's framing, subfloor, drywall and concrete using dehumidifiers, air movers and controlled temperature, then confirming dryness with moisture readings. It starts after standing water is extracted and continues until materials match the dry readings of unaffected areas.

Structural drying at a glance:

  • Dry target for wood framing: roughly 12 to 15% moisture content
  • Typical time: 3 to 5 days for most clean-water losses; 5 to 8 days for hardwood, cabinets or between-floor spaces
  • Key equipment: LGR dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, wall injection systems, drying mats
  • Proof of dryness: daily logs of temperature, humidity, grains per pound and material readings
  • Typical estimate: roughly $1,000 to $3,500 for one to two rooms

While Your Home Is Drying: 5 Rules That Speed It Up

  • Keep windows and outside doors closed. From spring through fall, Plano's outdoor air carries a lot of moisture and works against the dehumidifiers.
  • Leave the thermostat around 72 to 75°F (as long as your ductwork stayed dry). Steady warmth helps moisture leave materials.
  • Never turn the equipment off. Drying depends on nonstop airflow. One night off lets humidity rebound and stalls progress.
  • Open closets, vanities and pantry doors. Dry air has to reach the corners where moisture hides.
  • Check the dehumidifier drain hoses. Make sure hoses running to sinks, tubs or drains aren't kinked.

Why Fans and Your AC Can't Dry a Wet House in North Texas

Box fans only move moisture from wet materials into room air, and a home AC isn't designed to pull humidity low enough to dry studs and subfloor. Plano's outdoor air is humid much of the year, so after a leak, fans raise indoor humidity until evaporation slows and moisture settles back onto cool drywall, vents and north-facing walls.

Central AC is built for comfort, not drying. It can't bring the air down to the very low humidity that structural drying needs, and it can't push enough air along baseboards to pull water out of framing.

The 4 Principles of Drying (Made Simple)

1. Airflow Strips the Wet Layer Off Surfaces

Wet drywall and wood are wrapped in a thin layer of saturated air. Air movers sweep it away and replace it with drier air, so materials keep releasing water.

2. Dry Air Pulls Water Out of Wood

Moisture moves from wet to dry. Low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers dry the room air enough to pull moisture out of studs and subfloor, then condense and pump it away. They keep working at humidity levels where household dehumidifiers struggle.

3. Warmth Speeds Drying Up

A drying room held around 75 to 85°F helps moisture leave dense materials like oak flooring and concrete faster.

4. Some Materials Need Special Tools

Carpet and drywall respond to standard drying. Hardwood, plaster, brick and multi-layer subfloors hold more "bound" water and need drying mats, wall injection or desiccant dehumidifiers. See hardwood floor drying and wall cavity drying.

The 5-Step Structural Drying Process

Structural drying follows five steps: set a dry baseline, size the equipment, contain the drying area, log readings daily, and remove equipment only when readings prove the home is dry. Work follows recognized restoration practices (IICRC S500 guidance).

  • Set your home's dry baseline. Readings from an unaffected room in the same house show what "dry" means for your materials.
  • Size the equipment. Air movers and dehumidifiers are matched to the wet square footage, room volume and how much water materials absorbed.
  • Contain the drying area. Plastic containment closes off dry parts of the house so dehumidifiers focus on the wet zone, which helps drying move faster.
  • Log readings every 24 hours. Temperature, humidity and grains per pound going into and out of each dehumidifier are recorded, plus readings at marked spots on walls, baseboards and subfloors.
  • Remove equipment only when it's proven dry. Nothing leaves until test points reach dry standard: about 12 to 15% for framing, and in line with the baseline for drywall and subfloor.

Structural Drying FAQs

Why can't I just run my AC and ceiling fans?

Home AC is designed for comfort, not drying. It can't lower humidity enough or move enough air along baseboards to pull water out of studs before mold has a chance to start.

What does "grains per pound" mean on my drying log?

It's the weight of water vapor in a pound of air (7,000 grains equal one pound of water). When the air leaving a dehumidifier reads noticeably drier than the room air, the machine is actively removing water.

Why is the drying room so warm?

Dehumidifiers give off heat as they pull water from the air. A room around 75 to 85°F actually dries faster.

Why is my house still humid after the water was cleaned up?

The visible water is gone, but studs, subfloor and slab are still releasing moisture into the air. Structural drying removes exactly that.

Can the inside of a wall be dried without tearing it open?

Often, yes. On clean-water leaks, the baseboard is removed, small holes are drilled where trim will hide them, and dry air is injected into the wall cavity.

How do I know my home is really dry?

You get the readings. Equipment stays until every marked spot reaches dry standard, and the logs show progress day by day.

How long does structural drying take?

Most clean-water losses dry in 3 to 5 days. Hardwood, cabinets and between-floor cavities can take 5 to 8 days. Drying starts right after emergency cleanup and water extraction.
