What Is Flood Cleanup?

Flood cleanup is the removal of outside water that entered a home, along with mud, silt and every porous material it soaked, followed by cleaning, disinfecting and drying the structure. Because outside water carries soil bacteria and chemicals, it's treated as Category 3 (grossly contaminated) water from the start.

Flood cleanup at a glance:

  • Water category: Category 3 from the moment it crosses the ground outside
  • Must be removed: carpet, pad, and drywall and insulation the water touched
  • Can usually be saved: wood studs, concrete slab and well-bonded tile, after cleaning
  • Plano flood sources: creek overflow, poor yard grading, overwhelmed drains, clogged gutters
  • Insurance: rising surface water usually needs a separate flood policy
  • Typical estimate: roughly $2,000 to $5,000 for water through one door into one room

Storm Water Inside Your Home? Do These 5 Things

  • Treat it as contaminated. Any water that crossed soil, mulch, a patio or a street before coming in is Category 3. Keep kids and pets away.
  • Kill power before you step in. If water came over a patio threshold or garage door, shut off first-floor circuits at the main panel.
  • Photograph the water line. Before cleaning anything, photograph the outside grade, clogged drains and the water height on baseboards.
  • Clear outside drains once lightning passes. Pulling leaves and mulch off yard drains stops more water pushing through weep holes and door tracks.
  • Don't close up a wet room without dehumidifiers. Post-storm air in North Texas is humid enough that slabs and walls won't dry on their own.

Why Plano Floods: Creeks, Clay and Slab Height

Plano flooding is driven by creeks and stormwater runoff, not the coast. Heavy rain, saturated clay soil and slabs that sit only inches above the yard combine to push water inside.

Creeks Run Through the City

Spring Creek, Rowlett Creek, White Rock Creek, Russell Creek, Pitman Creek and others run through Plano. Upstream growth sends stormwater down these channels during heavy rain. The August 2022 storm, when parts of the Dallas area received around 10 inches or more of rain in less than a day, showed how fast streets and yards can overflow.

Slabs Sit Only Inches Above the Yard

Many Plano homes sit on slabs just a few inches above the lawn. Clogged gutters, poor backyard grading or an overwhelmed French drain let water pool against the house. Once it rises past the slab edge, it comes through brick weep holes and sliding door tracks.

Soaked Clay Stops Absorbing

After days of rain, Blackland Prairie clay can't absorb more. Sloped backyards in 75074, 75023, 75025 and 75093 can send runoff straight toward the house.

Why Flood Water Can't Be Dried Like a Pipe Leak

Outside water is contaminated, so drying alone isn't enough; materials it touched must be removed and surfaces disinfected first.

  • It's Category 3 from the start. Under IICRC S500 guidance, water entering from the ground outside is treated as grossly contaminated, even if it was clean rain moments earlier.
  • Porous materials must go. Carpet, pad and the bottom of drywall it touched can't be safely dried in place.
  • Mud hides in the walls. Silt settles on the bottom wood plate inside exterior walls, and wet insulation won't dry from fans blowing on painted drywall.

The Flood Cleanup Process

Flood cleanup follows five steps: pump out, contain, remove, disinfect and dry.

  • Pump it out. Gas-powered truck-mounted extractors and submersible pumps can work even when the neighborhood's power is out. See water extraction.
  • Contain the work area. Plastic barriers and HEPA air scrubbers keep contamination from spreading through the house.
  • Remove what the water touched. Carpet and pad come out, and drywall is cut 12 to 24 inches above the water line so wet insulation can be removed from exterior walls.
  • Clean, rinse and disinfect. Mud and silt are scrubbed off slabs and studs first, then surfaces are treated with disinfectant.
  • Dry everything to standard. Dehumidifiers and air movers run until studs read about 12 to 15%, logged daily. See structural drying.

Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Storm Flooding in Texas?

Usually not on its own: rising surface water and creek flooding typically need a separate flood policy (NFIP or private). Wind-driven rain coming through a storm-damaged roof or window is different and is often covered by a homeowners policy. Clear photos of where the water came in matter. Check your own policies.

Flood Cleanup FAQs

It was just rainwater from my yard. Why is it treated like sewage?

Once rain runs across lawn, mulch, patio or gutters, it picks up soil bacteria, animal waste, fertilizer and pesticides. Under IICRC S500 guidance, water entering from the ground outside is Category 3.

How did water get through my brick wall with the door closed?

Brick homes have small weep holes along the bottom row of brick. When water pools against the house, it can flow through them, over the slab edge and inside.

Can I just pull the wet pad and run fans on the wall?

No. Exterior walls hold insulation behind the drywall. Once dirty water soaks it, fans on the painted surface can't dry it or remove mud from the wood plate, and mold can follow within days.

My house isn't in a FEMA flood zone. Can it still flood?

Yes. Homes in lower-risk zones can still get water from street backups, overwhelmed drains and sheet flow during heavy downpours.

Can flood cleanup start if the power is out?

Yes. Gas-powered pumps and truck-mounted extractors don't need your home's electricity. Start with 24/7 emergency water damage cleanup.

How can I reduce flood risk around my Plano home?

Keep gutters and yard drains clear, make sure soil slopes away from the slab, extend downspouts, and consider flood insurance even outside high-risk zones.

What if sewage came up during the storm too?

Storms can overload sewer lines and push sewage into homes. That needs the same Category 3 process plus extra sanitizing. See sewage backup cleanup.
