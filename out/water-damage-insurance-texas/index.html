Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Texas?

Most standard Texas homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from plumbing, heating, AC, fire sprinkler systems and household appliances, but often exclude or limit damage from slow, long-term leaks, sewer backups without an endorsement, and rising floodwater. Policies also expect you to act quickly to prevent further damage.

Key points:

  • "Sudden and accidental" is the key phrase.
  • Slow leaks lasting weeks or months are often excluded or limited.
  • Adjusters look for timing clues: clean wood and one fresh water line suggest sudden; rot, corrosion and layered stains suggest long-term.
  • Duty to mitigate: you're expected to stop damage from getting worse.

Sudden vs. Gradual: What's the Difference?

Sudden water damage happens quickly from a one-time event, like a burst pipe or failed hose; gradual damage builds over weeks or months, like a slow drip, and is often excluded. Adjusters look at physical evidence to tell them apart:

  • Clean, light-colored wood vs. dark, rotted framing
  • One fresh water line vs. several crusty rings
  • A clean break in a pipe or hose vs. long-term corrosion
  • Whether you acted fast to stop the water and start drying

Your Duty to Mitigate

Texas homeowners policies generally expect you to take reasonable steps right away to keep damage from getting worse. That usually means shutting off the water, removing standing water and starting professional drying, even before the adjuster arrives. Waiting days can let a sudden leak grow into secondary damage like mold or rot, which may be harder to get covered. See the mold clock guide and shut-off valve guide.

How Good Documentation Protects Your Claim

Strong documentation shows the damage was sudden, that you acted fast, and why each removal or drying step was needed. It can include:

  • Timestamped photos of the untouched scene before anything is moved
  • The failed part photographed and saved: split pipe, burst hose, ruptured tank
  • Thermal images showing where water went. See moisture mapping.
  • Moisture readings on walls, floors and ceilings, room by room
  • Daily drying logs showing the home reached dry standard. See structural drying.
  • An itemized Xactimate-format scope, the format widely used by insurers

Understanding Your Deductible

Many Texas homeowners policies use a percentage deductible, like 1% or 2% of dwelling coverage, or a fixed amount. For small, quick leaks under your deductible, many homeowners pay directly. For larger losses with ceilings, floors or multiple rooms, filing often makes more sense. See the water damage cost guide.

Mistakes That Can Hurt a Claim

  • Waiting days to start drying
  • Throwing away the failed pipe, hose or part
  • Painting over stains before the area is tested dry
  • Not photographing the scene before cleanup
  • Letting a slow drip continue after noticing it

Texas Water Damage Insurance FAQs

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage in Texas?

Most standard policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from plumbing, appliances, AC and similar systems. Coverage depends on your specific policy.

What's the difference between sudden and gradual water damage?

Sudden damage happens quickly, like a burst pipe. Gradual damage builds over weeks or months, like a slow drip, and is often excluded.

How do I prove a leak was sudden?

Keep the failed part, photograph everything before cleanup, and get thermal images and moisture readings from day one.

Should I wait for the adjuster before drying?

Generally no. Policies expect you to prevent further damage promptly. Document first, then start drying.

Is sewer backup covered?

Often only with a water backup endorsement. Check your policy. See sewage backup cleanup.

Is creek or storm flooding covered?

Rising surface water usually needs a separate flood policy. See the storm water guide.

Are slab leaks covered in Texas?

Resulting water damage from a sudden, hidden slab leak is often covered, while slab access and pipe repair may be treated differently. See slab leak water damage.
